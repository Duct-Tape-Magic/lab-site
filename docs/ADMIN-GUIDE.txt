Welcome to OHS Chem Labs
========================

This is the site where our students do their labs. They open a lab, read the
procedure, type their data and answers straight into the page, add a photo or
two, and download a PDF of their report to turn in on Canvas. Everything you
need to run it is on the site itself. There is nothing to install and no code
to touch.

This guide walks you through the parts you will actually use. It takes about
ten minutes to read, and most of it will make sense the moment you see the
screens.


Getting in
----------
Go to ohschemlabs.com and click Log in at the top right. Sign in with your
Google account, or open "Admin sign in" and use your email and password. Once
you are in, a dark bar appears across the top of every page. That bar is
admin mode. Students never see it.

The bar has ten buttons. "+ Lab" and "+ Folder" create things. "Site text"
lets you reword anything students see. "Design" controls colours and layout.
"Widgets" lists the tools a lab can include (see below) and lets you switch
each one on or off. "Stats" shows who has been doing what. "Help" is where students' questions
arrive. "Classes" is where you make access codes. "Admins" is where you add
colleagues. And "View as student" shows you the site exactly as a student
sees it, which is the single most useful button when you are not sure how
something looks. A small pill in the corner brings you back.

If you leave the site alone for a day it signs you out. Just log in again.


The home page
-------------
The home page is a grid of folders and labs. A folder is just a container,
so you might have one per course or one per unit. In admin mode every card
gets Edit and Delete buttons and a pair of arrows. The arrows move a card
earlier or later in the grid, and folders and labs share one order, so a lab
can sit before a folder if you want it to.

Draft labs and admin-only folders show a little badge and are invisible to
students. When in doubt, click View as student.


Making a lab
------------
Click "+ Lab" and you get a form. The top half is housekeeping: a title, the
link name that becomes the lab's web address, the folder it lives in, a
one-line summary for the card, and two time estimates, one for the procedure
and an optional one for the write-up. Fill in what you have; most of it can
be changed later. The one thing to leave alone once a lab is live is the link
name, because students will have bookmarked it.

Status is where you decide who sees it. Draft means only admins. Published
means everyone who is allowed to open it, and "Who can open it" sets that:
everyone, anyone with a password, only certain classes and accounts, or only
admins. Most labs simply inherit whatever their folder says.

Lower down you can give the lab a cover picture for its card, a slideshow of
photos or videos, and files to download such as a PDF of the original
handout. Transparent PNGs look best as covers; the Zoom slider lets you make
a small icon and a big screenshot look the same size.

Then comes the part that matters, the procedure itself, which you build in
the Builder.


The Builder
-----------
The Builder shows your lab as a stack of blocks, in the order students will
read them. Think of each block as one thing on the page: a heading, a
paragraph, a table to fill in, a question with an answer box, a photo slot.
You add blocks with the row of buttons under the list, open a block by
clicking its little triangle, and move it with the arrows or by dragging the
handle on its left. The copy icon duplicates a block, the plus inserts one
after it, the eye jumps to it in the preview, and the cross deletes it.

A few blocks are worth knowing well. Text is for writing, and it understands
simple formatting: two hashes for a heading, stars around a word for bold,
and maths between dollar signs, so $\Delta H$ comes out as a proper delta.
Numbered steps is for the procedure proper; every step gets its own notes
box for students. Data table is a table where you choose which cells are
blank for students to fill in and which are fixed, and you can let them add
rows. Question is a prompt with an answer box, and under "More options" you
can give it a heading, a hint, a required flag or a word target. Photo slot
is where a student adds a picture of their work. Picture is a picture you
supply, in three sizes, and small or medium pictures placed one after another
line up side by side. Graph draws a live chart from one of your data tables
and fills in as students type. Callout gives you the coloured safety, warning
and tip boxes.

Widget… adds a tool that uses the student's device. The first one is a
colorimeter: it opens the phone camera and shows two small squares the
student drags into place, one onto a plain matte object that will stay in the
same light all lab (the reference: a grey card or a sheet of paper is better
than anything white) and one onto the sample. The student taps Set reference
once; from then on the sample's red, green and blue values are shown live,
corrected for any change in the light or in the phone's automatic exposure
and white balance since that moment. When the student taps Record, those
three numbers go into one of your data tables as a new row, so they save,
sync, print and review exactly like typed numbers. On the widget's card you
choose the table, which columns take R, G and B, and optionally columns for
the uncorrected camera values; students type sample names or concentrations
into the table's other columns themselves. A green circled check pops onto
Set reference or Record when the action succeeds, then fades. Put the widget right
after the table it fills, and let that table add rows.
Nothing happens until a student taps Start, so nobody reading on a laptop
gets a camera prompt; a laptop without a camera just shows a note to open
the lab on a phone. If the camera cannot start, the student sees a short
note, the table to type into, and a Get help button if the lab has Get help
on. The tool shows no text of its own, only the picture, the two squares,
the numbers and its buttons; the Description field on its card is shown
under the Colorimeter title in that lab, so put the guidance there: which
object is the reference, keep it and the sample in the same light, keep the
lamp still, and avoid readings near 0 or 255.
The Widgets button in the admin bar shows every available tool with a demo.

Below the Procedure list is a second list called Report. Anything you put
there gives students a Report tab with a guided write-up, and the "Include"
buttons let you pull a table, a graph, an answer or a photo from the
procedure into the report so students see their own data in context.

Under the Builder is a preview that shows the lab exactly as students will
see it and updates as you type. Click Refresh if it ever lags behind.

One rule to remember: every block that stores student work has a permanent
name, shown on the right of its card. The site assigns these when you first
save. On a lab that students have already started, do not rename them,
because their saved answers are attached to those names. The site will warn
you if a change would hide anyone's work, and rewording, reordering and
adding blocks are always safe.

Click Save at the bottom when you are done. The site keeps a copy of the
previous version every time you save.

If you have a lab as a PDF, there is a shortcut: Claude can convert it into
the site's lab-file format using the guide in the repository, and the Lab
file view has an Import button that turns that file into blocks.


Folders
-------
Folders work much like labs: a name, a link name, a description, an optional
cover picture, an order number, and the same "Who can open it" choice.
Folders can sit inside folders. The usual arrangement is one folder per
course, set to "Selected student accounts" with the right classes ticked, so
every lab inside is open to that course and closed to everyone else.


Classes and access codes
------------------------
Students do not make accounts, and the site never stores their names or
emails. Instead you create a class, and the site makes one access code for
every seat in it. Open Classes, click New class, give it a name, a short
prefix such as P3, and the number of seats, and the codes are ready.

Open the class to see them. Show reveals a code; Copy list copies all of them
so you can mail-merge them from your own email. A student clicks Log in, then
Access Code Login, and types their code. It works on as many devices as they
like, and everything they type follows the code. You can give a seat a
nickname that only you see, issue a new code if one gets shared (the old one
stops working immediately), add seats mid-term, and at the end of term close
the class, which keeps all the work but stops logins. Please close classes
rather than deleting them.


Students' work, and keeping it safe
-----------------------------------
Everything a logged-in student types is saved to their account as they type,
and so are their photos. A cleared browser, a crashed laptop or a new device
makes no difference; they log in and it is all there. Someone browsing
without logging in keeps their work in the browser only.

You can look at any student's saved work. Stats, then Students, lists
accounts; Classes lists seats. Either one opens a read-only copy of a
student's entries for a lab, photos included, and can export a spreadsheet.

The site is built to never lose student work through an ordinary action, and
it will stop and ask before anything risky. If a warning ever mentions
students' saved work, read it twice.


Get help
--------
Tick "Get help" in a lab's form and a Get help button appears at the top of
that lab. A logged-in student can type a question and send it. Within seconds
the admins you ticked get an email, the Help button in the admin bar shows a
count, and the browser tab title shows it too. Open Help, click the thread,
and reply. The student sees your answer on their lab page immediately, or
waiting in their thread next time they open the lab. Mark it resolved when
you are done; they can still write back.

You can also write an automatic reply that students see the moment they send,
so they know someone has been notified.


Words, colours and layout
-------------------------
Site text is where every label, button and message lives, grouped by page.
If you want the site to say "TA" instead of "teacher", that is where you
change it.

Design has three tabs. Palette sets the site's default colours. Cards sets
how the home grid looks. Layout sets page width, reading width, whether the
lab column is centred, and how much space sits above a lab. Everything
previews live; Save publishes it, Cancel undoes it, and Reset brings back the
original look.

Separately, the three little boxes in the footer let anyone pick Original,
Paper or Graphite, which is dark mode, for themselves. That choice is
personal and follows a student between devices.


When something looks odd
------------------------
Hard-refresh first: hold Shift and click reload, or press Cmd+Shift+R. Every
change to the site goes out as a new version, and an old cached copy explains
most oddities. If a yellow banner says the site is showing a saved copy, the
database could not be reached for a moment; wait a minute and reload, and do
not edit labs until it clears.
